## P7-C2 — A minimum-curvature line that converges

State: done.

- **Solver.** The line's offsets minimise Σ|p(i−1) − 2p(i) + p(i+1)|², its squared
  curvature summed on the 2 m spacing, within ±(half width − car clearance). With
  points moving only along the centreline's normals, that is exactly a convex
  quadratic programme with box bounds. Its Hessian is the cyclic pentadiagonal DᵀD
  (6, −4, 1), weighted by how neighbouring normals align.
  - ADMM (ρ = 1, 400 iterations) uses one banded factorisation and many O(n) solves.
    It brings the offsets near the answer and finds which points sit at an edge.
  - An active-set polish then fixes those points, solves the rest exactly, and moves
    points on or off the edges by the KKT conditions until nothing changes.
  - The closed loop's wrap is handled by splitting off two unknowns: an LDLᵀ band plus
    a 2×2 Schur complement.
- **Result**, old (P6-C6 Gauss–Seidel) against new:

  | Track      | Room   | Median offset (old → new) | 90th percentile | Estimated lap (old → new) | Centreline |
  | ---------- | ------ | ------------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------- | ---------- |
  | Harbour    | 4.70 m | 0.07 → 4.20 m             | 0.85 → 4.70 m   | 71.4 → 67.1 s             | 78.0 s     |
  | Riviera    | 3.70 m | 0.38 → 2.12 m             | 2.26 → 3.67 m   | 83.8 → 82.7 s             | 96.9 s     |
  | Ardennes   | 5.20 m | 0.01 → 3.21 m             | 0.05 → 5.19 m   | 132.6 → 121.2 s           | 137.8 s    |
  | Royal Park | 5.20 m | 0.04 → 4.36 m             | 0.72 → 5.20 m   | 98.7 → 96.0 s             | 104.4 s    |
  | Corniche   | 4.70 m | 0.02 → 2.71 m             | 0.08 → 4.65 m   | 119.8 → 109.4 s           | 129.6 s    |
  | Test Loop  | 4.70 m | 0.01 → 3.53 m             | 0.11 → 4.70 m   | 27.9 → 23.8 s             | 29.7 s     |

  Build time on this machine (busy, load about 20): 197 ms for Ardennes (3,477
  points), under 120 ms for Harbour.

- **Speed profile.** P6-C6's profile already planned on a friction circle whose radius
  grows with downforce (a g-g-v envelope): corner limit `v²κ ≤ μ(g + k·v²/m)`,
  forward on power, backward on brakes, each with the grip left over from cornering.
  It is kept as it is. `estimatedLapTimeS` now reports its lap time.
- **Corrected premise.** The plan expected the line to touch the inside edge at a 90°
  corner's apex. The converged optimum of this objective does not on an isolated
  corner: the squared-curvature sum is lowest when the turn is spread over the
  straights either side, like an elastica. On a 12 m road with 20 m corners, it runs
  on the outside edge 90 m either side and reaches into the inside half at the apex
  (−2.4 of ±4.7 m), turning on a 26 m radius where the road turns on 20 m. The
  kerb-clipping late apex is a minimum-time trait, which is C3's job. The test states
  what the objective gives, and KKT was checked on that track: the free-point gradient
  is under 1e-8.
- **Tests:**
  - the square: outside edge before and after, inside half at the apex, radius over
    24 m;
  - a small track: bends within 1% of a projected coordinate descent run until nothing
    moves. The old solver was 26% worse; the new one is 0.01% better;
  - every shipped track: estimated lap beats the centreline's, the line never leaves
    the band, and the build is quick (a loose 3 s bound);
  - the existing line tests (bounds, less bending, continuity, determinism, profile
    and guide) are unchanged and pass.
- Checked on screen: from the grid, the line crosses to the left edge for the
  right-hand Turn 1.

## P7-C3 — Minimum-time refinement

- **Method.** C2's minimum-curvature line is the starting point. On top of it, a
  descent on the estimated lap time itself:
  - the lap time's gradient comes from reverse mode through the speed profile's
    forward and backward passes, giving its sensitivity to each point's curvature and
    step length. It is chained to the offsets through the circle curvature over ±2
    samples;
  - each step is preconditioned by the curvature Hessian (plus a small damping), so it
    moves the line smoothly rather than point by point. Points at an edge that the
    gradient pushes further out are held there;
  - an adaptive line search (first step at most 0.5 m, halved up to 12 times, grown
    ×1.5 after a success) accepts a step only if the lap time falls. So the time never
    rises; `lapTimesS` records every iteration's time;
  - 80 iterations by default; `{ iterations: 0 }` gives C2's line.
- **Friction-circle softening.** At the grip limit, the grip left over from cornering,
  `√(grip² − lateral²)`, has an infinite slope, which broke the gradient at every
  corner's slowest point. It is softened by ε = 0.5 m/s²: still zero at the limit,
  with a finite slope. It changes C2's estimated laps by under 0.3 s. The gradient was
  checked against finite differences to within about 1%.
- **Result.** Estimated lap, minimum-curvature → refined, and build time on this
  machine:

  | Track      | Estimated lap     | Build  |
  | ---------- | ----------------- | ------ |
  | Harbour    | 67.25 → 65.71 s   | 262 ms |
  | Riviera    | 83.12 → 77.80 s   | 204 ms |
  | Ardennes   | 121.49 → 120.34 s | 411 ms |
  | Royal Park | 96.20 → 91.87 s   | 352 ms |
  | Corniche   | 109.64 → 104.36 s | 366 ms |
  | Test Loop  | 23.86 → 23.41 s   | 68 ms  |

  Builds now take up to 0.4 s, over the plan's 200 ms for C2 alone. The line is built
  once, when a track loads, so this was accepted rather than traded for lap time.

- **Corrected premise.** The plan expected corners before long straights to apex
  later. Measured on a symmetric corner, the refined apex moves only 0.5 m later in
  this point-mass model. What the time gradient does buy is the classic geometric
  line: it clips the inside at the apex, which minimum curvature leaves open. On the
  square it reaches 2.56 m inside with minimum curvature and 4.20 m of the 4.7 m room
  refined. The test states that instead.
- **Tests:**
  - every shipped track: `lapTimesS` never rises, and falls overall; the reported
    estimate is its last entry; `{ iterations: 0 }` gives just the first;
  - the square: minimum curvature reaches under 0.6 of the room at the apex, the
    refined line over 0.85;
  - C2's brute-force and square tests now pin `{ iterations: 0 }`, since they describe
    the minimum-curvature objective;
  - continuity is restated as the refined line allows: the seam step is no bigger than
    the largest step, the largest step is under 1 m, and heading kinks are under
    0.2 rad. The old fixed numbers were tuned to the smoother C2 line;
  - the corner-slowing test also accepts a car still accelerating 300 m before a
    corner, which the faster refined line now does on one track.
- Gates: `make lint` passes; `make test` 536 unit tests and 42/42 Chromium browser
  tests.

## P7-C4 — A 3D, dynamic racing line

- **`guidance(input, line, limits)`** (`src/simulation/guidance.ts`) is pure. Each
  frame it plans the next 300 m of line from the car's state now:
  - each point's target is the line's speed, lowered to what the grip available now
    holds through that point's curvature. Braking back from those targets gives an
    envelope;
  - the car is run forward from its own speed, on full power where it can be, and
    otherwise on just the throttle or brake that keeps it under the envelope. That
    pedal, from full throttle (+1) to full brake (−1), is each point's colour;
  - a lift (yellow) is shown for throttle under 20%, and over the 0.6 s of travel
    before each braking zone;
  - grip now is `gripShare`: the tyres' mean surface grip against clean road, cut to
    0.75 for a slipping tyre (the vehicle's own sliding figure). The session reports
    it on every frame;
  - off the line, the guide bends from the car back to it along a smoothstep, over
    1.5 s of travel, kept within 60–120 m.

  It costs about 0.03 ms a call (Harbour, 1,000 calls).

- **View.** The guide is a ribbon raised 0.12 m, with darker sides, plus a chevron
  every 8 m pointing the way. Green deepens with throttle and red with brake. The
  brake red keeps its green channel above the screenshot tests' car threshold.
  "Braking zones" mode still draws only the lift and brake parts.
- **Replaced.** P6-C6's `guideColour` (a phase per point from the car's speed alone)
  and its four tests are removed; `guidance` covers the same behaviour with a car
  model. Its "a slower car is never told to brake early" case is kept as a guidance
  test.
- **Tests** (at the `guidance` seam, on Harbour's heaviest braking zone, from 200 m
  out):
  - at the line's speed: throttle, then a lift, then brake within 8 m of where the
    profile brakes, with a full pedal;
  - 12 m/s faster: it brakes more than 20 m earlier, with over 10% more brake work;
  - half speed: it brakes no earlier, and less;
  - 60% grip: it brakes more than 20 m sooner;
  - 3 m off the line: the gap starts at 3 m, never grows, and closes at 55–65 m at
    30 m/s and 110–125 m at 80 m/s. On the line, the guide is the line;
  - colours ramp with the pedal; `gripShare` on the grid is 1, and in the gravel it is
    at most the track's gravel grip (a session test);
  - browser: the ribbon is raised over 0.1 m and has more than 30 chevrons.
- Checked on screen (High, headless): green from the grid, red braking into Turn 1 at
  205 km/h, and the ribbon curving back from a car off the line.
