## P7-C2 — A minimum-curvature line that converges

State: done.

- **Solver.** The line's offsets minimise Σ|p(i−1) − 2p(i) + p(i+1)|², its squared
  curvature summed on the 2 m spacing, within ±(half width − car clearance). With
  points moving only along the centreline's normals, that is exactly a convex
  quadratic programme with box bounds. Its Hessian is the cyclic pentadiagonal DᵀD
  (6, −4, 1), weighted by how neighbouring normals align.
  - ADMM (ρ = 1, 400 iterations) uses one banded factorisation and many O(n) solves.
    It brings the offsets near the answer and finds which points sit at an edge.
  - An active-set polish then fixes those points, solves the rest exactly, and moves
    points on or off the edges by the KKT conditions until nothing changes.
  - The closed loop's wrap is handled by splitting off two unknowns: an LDLᵀ band plus
    a 2×2 Schur complement.
- **Result**, old (P6-C6 Gauss–Seidel) against new:

  | Track      | Room   | Median offset (old → new) | 90th percentile | Estimated lap (old → new) | Centreline |
  | ---------- | ------ | ------------------------- | --------------- | ------------------------- | ---------- |
  | Harbour    | 4.70 m | 0.07 → 4.20 m             | 0.85 → 4.70 m   | 71.4 → 67.1 s             | 78.0 s     |
  | Riviera    | 3.70 m | 0.38 → 2.12 m             | 2.26 → 3.67 m   | 83.8 → 82.7 s             | 96.9 s     |
  | Ardennes   | 5.20 m | 0.01 → 3.21 m             | 0.05 → 5.19 m   | 132.6 → 121.2 s           | 137.8 s    |
  | Royal Park | 5.20 m | 0.04 → 4.36 m             | 0.72 → 5.20 m   | 98.7 → 96.0 s             | 104.4 s    |
  | Corniche   | 4.70 m | 0.02 → 2.71 m             | 0.08 → 4.65 m   | 119.8 → 109.4 s           | 129.6 s    |
  | Test Loop  | 4.70 m | 0.01 → 3.53 m             | 0.11 → 4.70 m   | 27.9 → 23.8 s             | 29.7 s     |

  Build time on this machine (busy, load about 20): 197 ms for Ardennes (3,477
  points), under 120 ms for Harbour.

- **Speed profile.** P6-C6's profile already planned on a friction circle whose radius
  grows with downforce (a g-g-v envelope): corner limit `v²κ ≤ μ(g + k·v²/m)`,
  forward on power, backward on brakes, each with the grip left over from cornering.
  It is kept as it is. `estimatedLapTimeS` now reports its lap time.
- **Corrected premise.** The plan expected the line to touch the inside edge at a 90°
  corner's apex. The converged optimum of this objective does not on an isolated
  corner: the squared-curvature sum is lowest when the turn is spread over the
  straights either side, like an elastica. On a 12 m road with 20 m corners, it runs
  on the outside edge 90 m either side and reaches into the inside half at the apex
  (−2.4 of ±4.7 m), turning on a 26 m radius where the road turns on 20 m. The
  kerb-clipping late apex is a minimum-time trait, which is C3's job. The test states
  what the objective gives, and KKT was checked on that track: the free-point gradient
  is under 1e-8.
- **Tests:**
  - the square: outside edge before and after, inside half at the apex, radius over
    24 m;
  - a small track: bends within 1% of a projected coordinate descent run until nothing
    moves. The old solver was 26% worse; the new one is 0.01% better;
  - every shipped track: estimated lap beats the centreline's, the line never leaves
    the band, and the build is quick (a loose 3 s bound);
  - the existing line tests (bounds, less bending, continuity, determinism, profile
    and guide) are unchanged and pass.
- Checked on screen: from the grid, the line crosses to the left edge for the
  right-hand Turn 1.

## P7-C3 — Minimum-time refinement

- **Method.** C2's minimum-curvature line is the starting point. On top of it, a
  descent on the estimated lap time itself:
  - the lap time's gradient comes from reverse mode through the speed profile's
    forward and backward passes, giving its sensitivity to each point's curvature and
    step length. It is chained to the offsets through the circle curvature over ±2
    samples;
  - each step is preconditioned by the curvature Hessian (plus a small damping), so it
    moves the line smoothly rather than point by point. Points at an edge that the
    gradient pushes further out are held there;
  - an adaptive line search (first step at most 0.5 m, halved up to 12 times, grown
    ×1.5 after a success) accepts a step only if the lap time falls. So the time never
    rises; `lapTimesS` records every iteration's time;
  - 80 iterations by default; `{ iterations: 0 }` gives C2's line.
- **Friction-circle softening.** At the grip limit, the grip left over from cornering,
  `√(grip² − lateral²)`, has an infinite slope, which broke the gradient at every
  corner's slowest point. It is softened by ε = 0.5 m/s²: still zero at the limit,
  with a finite slope. It changes C2's estimated laps by under 0.3 s. The gradient was
  checked against finite differences to within about 1%.
- **Result.** Estimated lap, minimum-curvature → refined, and build time on this
  machine:

  | Track      | Estimated lap     | Build  |
  | ---------- | ----------------- | ------ |
  | Harbour    | 67.25 → 65.71 s   | 262 ms |
  | Riviera    | 83.12 → 77.80 s   | 204 ms |
  | Ardennes   | 121.49 → 120.34 s | 411 ms |
  | Royal Park | 96.20 → 91.87 s   | 352 ms |
  | Corniche   | 109.64 → 104.36 s | 366 ms |
  | Test Loop  | 23.86 → 23.41 s   | 68 ms  |

  Builds now take up to 0.4 s, over the plan's 200 ms for C2 alone. The line is built
  once, when a track loads, so this was accepted rather than traded for lap time.

- **Corrected premise.** The plan expected corners before long straights to apex
  later. Measured on a symmetric corner, the refined apex moves only 0.5 m later in
  this point-mass model. What the time gradient does buy is the classic geometric
  line: it clips the inside at the apex, which minimum curvature leaves open. On the
  square it reaches 2.56 m inside with minimum curvature and 4.20 m of the 4.7 m room
  refined. The test states that instead.
- **Tests:**
  - every shipped track: `lapTimesS` never rises, and falls overall; the reported
    estimate is its last entry; `{ iterations: 0 }` gives just the first;
  - the square: minimum curvature reaches under 0.6 of the room at the apex, the
    refined line over 0.85;
  - C2's brute-force and square tests now pin `{ iterations: 0 }`, since they describe
    the minimum-curvature objective;
  - continuity is restated as the refined line allows: the seam step is no bigger than
    the largest step, the largest step is under 1 m, and heading kinks are under
    0.2 rad. The old fixed numbers were tuned to the smoother C2 line;
  - the corner-slowing test also accepts a car still accelerating 300 m before a
    corner, which the faster refined line now does on one track.
- Gates: `make lint` passes; `make test` 536 unit tests and 42/42 Chromium browser
  tests.

## P7-C4 — A 3D, dynamic racing line

- **`guidance(input, line, limits)`** (`src/simulation/guidance.ts`) is pure. Each
  frame it plans the next 300 m of line from the car's state now:
  - each point's target is the line's speed, lowered to what the grip available now
    holds through that point's curvature. Braking back from those targets gives an
    envelope;
  - the car is run forward from its own speed, on full power where it can be, and
    otherwise on just the throttle or brake that keeps it under the envelope. That
    pedal, from full throttle (+1) to full brake (−1), is each point's colour;
  - a lift (yellow) is shown for throttle under 20%, and over the 0.6 s of travel
    before each braking zone;
  - grip now is `gripShare`: the tyres' mean surface grip against clean road, cut to
    0.75 for a slipping tyre (the vehicle's own sliding figure). The session reports
    it on every frame;
  - off the line, the guide bends from the car back to it along a smoothstep, over
    1.5 s of travel, kept within 60–120 m.

  It costs about 0.03 ms a call (Harbour, 1,000 calls).

- **View.** The guide is a ribbon raised 0.12 m, with darker sides, plus a chevron
  every 8 m pointing the way. Green deepens with throttle and red with brake. The
  brake red keeps its green channel above the screenshot tests' car threshold.
  "Braking zones" mode still draws only the lift and brake parts.
- **Replaced.** P6-C6's `guideColour` (a phase per point from the car's speed alone)
  and its four tests are removed; `guidance` covers the same behaviour with a car
  model. Its "a slower car is never told to brake early" case is kept as a guidance
  test.
- **Tests** (at the `guidance` seam, on Harbour's heaviest braking zone, from 200 m
  out):
  - at the line's speed: throttle, then a lift, then brake within 8 m of where the
    profile brakes, with a full pedal;
  - 12 m/s faster: it brakes more than 20 m earlier, with over 10% more brake work;
  - half speed: it brakes no earlier, and less;
  - 60% grip: it brakes more than 20 m sooner;
  - 3 m off the line: the gap starts at 3 m, never grows, and closes at 55–65 m at
    30 m/s and 110–125 m at 80 m/s. On the line, the guide is the line;
  - colours ramp with the pedal; `gripShare` on the grid is 1, and in the gravel it is
    at most the track's gravel grip (a session test);
  - browser: the ribbon is raised over 0.1 m and has more than 30 chevrons.
- Checked on screen (High, headless): green from the grid, red braking into Turn 1 at
  205 km/h, and the ribbon curving back from a car off the line.
- **Benchmark.** `make bench QUALITY=high LOADED=1` (full line, ghost, far chase), 3

## P7-C5 — Faster, headless simulation

- **Measure.** `make sim-speed` (`tools/sim-speed.ts`) runs the benchmark autopilot
  round Harbour with the energy system on for 120 simulated seconds, three times, and
  prints simulated seconds per wall-clock second. Before and after were run
  back-to-back on the same machine:

  | Build                         | Runs           | Median   |
  | ----------------------------- | -------------- | -------- |
  | Before (`940416c`)            | 75, 75, 78×    | **75×**  |
  | Barrier field only (60 s)     | 329, 356, 392× | 356×     |
  | After (field, snapshot cache) | 533, 564, 596× | **564×** |

- **Profile first** (`bun --cpu-prof`). 72% of the time was inside Rapier's
  JavaScript `World.step`. After every step it walks every collider in the world to
  keep its handle map current (`mapNewSoftBodies` → `mapNewColliders`,
  `unmapRemovedColliders`), calling back into JavaScript once per collider. Harbour's
  barriers are one box per 2 m segment on each side, a few thousand colliders. With
  the barriers removed, the same run did 448×.
- **Fix 1: `createBarrierField`** (`src/simulation/barrier-field.ts`). The segments
  are indexed in a 16 m grid, and a pool of box colliders is kept only on those
  within 20 m of the car. They are re-placed after the car moves 4 m, so every
  segment within 16 m is always solid, over ten steps of travel at top speed. Each
  box has exactly the size, place and friction its segment had before. The pool
  grows if a place needs more boxes.
- **Fix 2: one snapshot per step.** The session, the mark recorder and the autopilot
  each took `snapshot()` every step, and each rebuilt it (29% of the time after
  fix 1). It is now built once and cleared by `step`, `reset` and every setter.
- **Same physics.** Autopilot laps of Harbour, Riviera and Corniche give the same lap
  times and final positions to the last digit, before and after
  (113.27495869035599 s, 149.69586657602773 s, 204.16835765053384 s).
- The plan's second candidate, the mark recorder reading Rapier per wheel, came to
  under 2% in the profile; it reads the shared snapshot now. What remains is Rapier's
  own step (about 35%) and the autopilot's own full-lap `locate` (17%), which is the
  test driver's cost, not the simulation's.
- **Tests:**
  - a wall of 600 two-metre segments 300 m down the road stops the car at its face
    (it would pass through if only the boxes near the start were solid);
  - the existing barrier tests pass (a single wall, the drawn face, and the Turn 1
    gravel trap held by its barrier);
  - a snapshot taken between steps shows each changed setting (assists, wing, a step,
    a reset).
- **Tyre model.** [ADR-004](../decisions/ADR-004-tyre-model.md): the slip-based model
  is not built in P7. Rapier's controller can't take an external tyre law, so it
  needs our own raycast vehicle, with sub-steps for low-speed stability. That would
  give back much of this throughput, and it retunes every envelope. It would be its
  own phase, with the user's agreement.
- Gates: `make lint` passes; `make test` 545 unit tests and 42/42 Chromium browser
  tests.

## P7-C6 — External control API

- **Protocol** ([CONTROL](../CONTROL.md), `controlVersion` 1): `reset`, `step` and
  `close` messages in the shape of a Gym environment.
  - Replies carry the observation: pose, velocity, yaw rate, gear and rpm, per-wheel
    slip and surface; track distance, lateral offset and heading error; 32 samples of
    curvature and barrier room 10 m apart; lap and sector state; energy.
  - Steps also report events (`sector`, `lap`, `offTrack`, `backOnTrack`), and
    `done` at the episode's lap or time limit.
- **Headless:** `tools/sim-server.ts`, one JSON line in and one out, in order. Each
  reset builds a fresh session (about 70 ms), so an episode depends only on its
  messages. A step message of 3 steps takes about 0.2 ms in-process and 0.3 ms round
  trip through the pipes.
- **Browser:** `?control` puts `window.formulaRacer.control.send` on the page, over
  the game's own session. While outside software has the car, frames only draw it
  (a zero-second frame now simulates nothing), and the keyboard takes over again
  after `close`.
- **Session:** `drive(controls)` runs one simulation step with analog controls, sharing
  the per-step body (countdown, aero, timing, ghost, marks) with keyboard frames. The
  autopilot laps are bit-identical after the refactor. `wheelSurfaces()` reports the
  ground under each tyre.
- **Found on the way:** the first version of the server never exited after `close`,
  because readline kept stdin open. The example client then waited on it forever.
  The server test now asserts that the process exits.
- **Tests:**
  - reset starts at the green light, on the road, with the settings asked for;
  - the same reset and 40 step messages give identical replies twice;
  - a step advances exactly its steps; time limits end the episode, and only reset
    starts another;
  - 14 kinds of malformed message each get an error reply, and the episode carries
    on;
  - driving off the road gives `offTrack`;
  - the server answers in order, errors included (bad JSON, a step before reset),
    and exits on close;
  - the example client in the docs drives a Harbour lap through the server
    (108.4 s, with two sector events and a lap);
  - browser: `?control` drives the page's car, the keyboard is ignored until

- Gates: `make lint` passes; `make test` 557 unit tests and 44/44 Chromium browser

## P7-C7 — AI drivers with levels

- **Driver** (`src/ai/driver.ts`). It drives from the control API's observation, the
  same one outside software gets:
  - **Steering:** pure pursuit on its racing line, looking ahead a level-set number of
    seconds of travel (8–45 m).
  - **Pedals:** from `guidance` (C4), the throttle or brake the car needs now to stay
    under the speeds its line can carry. The throttle opens at a level-set rate.
  - **Off its line:** it plans on less grip the further it strays (from 1 m off), so it
    slows and comes back.
  - **Stuck:** stopped on the throttle for a second (nose in a wall), it reverses out
    with the wheel turned the other way, stops, selects first and drives on.

## P7-C8 — AI runs improve the line

- **The problem the runs found.** Ace on Riviera ran wide at the hairpin every lap,
  onto the run-off and into the wall. One tyre stayed on the road, so the lap rule's
  offTrack event never fired, and C7's tests passed. Traced step by step:
  - At full lock and full brake, Ace arrived at 17 m/s where the line planned 11.8.
  - On the way in, it braked at 7.1 m/s² where the plan allowed 11.9.
  - Measured at 20 m/s:
    - braking alone reaches 14.8 m/s², and cornering alone 19.8;
    - at 9.2 m/s² of cornering, braking reaches 11.3, where the plan's friction circle
      gives 13.1;
    - near the cornering limit, braking gives 2.
  - So the car's combined grip is a superellipse (exponent about 1.5), not the circle
    the profile plans on. Where the line asks for hard braking while turning in, the
    car does not have it.
- **A per-point grip scale on the line.** `buildRacingLine` takes
  `{ gripScale }`, one factor per centreline sample. The profile plans each point on
  the planned grip times its factor, and `guidance` reads the same factors from the
  line. A scale of 1 everywhere gives exactly the plain line.
- **The tuning** (`src/ai/line-tuning.ts`, `make tune-lines`):
  - Each round rebuilds the line on the scale and drives three Ace laps headless
    through the control API. The measure is the mean of the two flying laps.
  - **Lower.** While any tyre leaves the road and kerbs, the scale is cut by 10% from
    150 m before each such place to 20 m after it, the braking zone that set up the
    mistake.
  - **Restore.** Once clean, the lowered points are raised back toward 1, by half the
    way and then by halves. This finds the smallest cut that keeps the car on.
  - A round is kept only if better: clean beats not clean, then the faster lap (or,
    while not clean, fewer steps off the road). Everything is deterministic.
  - The tool saves `public/assets/lines/<track>.json`: the lowered stretches as runs,
    plus both measured laps. It writes only when the new line measures better than the
    saved one. Rerunning it saves nothing.
- **Results** (Ace, mean flying lap):

  | Track      | Plain line                   | Saved line | Lowered              |
  | ---------- | ---------------------------- | ---------- | -------------------- |
  | Riviera    | 78.016 s, 314 steps off road | 77.198 s   | 93 samples, to 0.925 |
  | Harbour    | 66.431 s                     | same       | none                 |
  | Ardennes   | 120.942 s                    | same       | none                 |
  | Royal Park | 92.407 s                     | same       | none                 |
  | Corniche   | 104.476 s                    | same       | none                 |
  | Test Loop  | 23.074 s                     | same       | none                 |

  The whole tool takes 18 s for six tracks.

- **Raising grip was tried and measured slower, so the tool does not do it.**
  - Raised everywhere by 5%, Ace put tyres off on both Harbour and Riviera.
  - Raised by 1–4% where the car held, the clean laps were 0.2–1.2 s slower.
  - The planned grip is below what steady cornering shows (C7). But the rest goes to
    braking while turning and to the driver's corrections, so there is none spare.
- **Also measured:** lap-time noise. A 0.03% change in grip moves a lap by up to
  0.4 s on a clean track. So the tool compares whole runs and keeps a change only when
  it measures better; it does not follow small gradients.
- **Loading:**
  - The game reads the track's saved line, if there is one, and builds both the guide
    and the `I` driver's line on it.

## Review fixes after P7-C8 (PR #7, Codex)

- **Energy only moves through the tyres.** Physics is now p3.11. The ERS is capped by
  what the rear tyres can pass this step:
  - **Deployment:** what the tyres grip beyond the engine's drive, below traction
    control's cap. On a surface at 0.15 grip it had deployed 7.5 times what all four
    tyres could pass.
  - **Lift-off harvesting:** the same limit applies. It was 2.8 times over.
  - **Braking regen:** a locked tyre harvests only its sliding grip.
  - As a result, a launch deploys nothing until about 130 km/h (3.2 s), where the rear
    tyres first have grip to spare. It used to drain energy there that traction control
    then threw away.
  - Two old test thresholds had encoded that waste:
    - "5 s flat out drains 1 MJ" now reads 500 kJ;
    - the smoke tests' "under 97% after 2 s" now reads "under full after 3.8 s".
  - Saved lines were re-measured with `make tune-lines`, which now refreshes a kept
    line's laps. Riviera re-tuned to 77.083 s (scale 0.9125); the plain line runs
    78.673 s with 405 steps off the road.
- **Content is bounded at load:**
  - track points to ±2500 m, inside the ±3000 m ground;
  - at most 2000 control points and 20 aero zones;
  - kerbs to 3 m;
  - power and deployment curves to 64 points.
- **Laps:** benchmark and AI-driven laps are never the player's records. The benchmark
  keeps its laps in memory, and any lap the AI or outside software drove part of is
  skipped. A ghost is used only with the best lap it belongs to.
- **Bench distance** is summed from the car's drawn positions, so long frames do not
  inflate it.
- **Keys:** Escape pauses from a focused dropdown.
- **Scenery:**
  - TecPro blocks sit in the wall, 1 cm proud of the collider face.
  - Gantry and bridge legs stand behind any barrier.
- **AI wall recovery in a manual box:** the driver brakes and shifts down, one gear per
  0.4 s, until it is in reverse, and only then backs out. Before, it asked for one
  downshift and applied throttle in whatever gear it had. From 6th it never reached
  reverse and got away only by luck. The wall test now runs in both boxes and requires
  reverse.
