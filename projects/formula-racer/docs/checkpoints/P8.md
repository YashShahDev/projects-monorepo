## P8-C0 — Baseline and budgets

### GPU time in the bench

A locked 60 Hz shows only that the GPU keeps up, not how much it has spare, so the
bench now reports GPU time. `src/app/gpu-timer.ts` wraps each bench frame's render in
an `EXT_disjoint_timer_query_webgl2` query. Results arrive a few frames late. At most
8 queries are in flight, and frames the GPU marked disjoint are dropped. The report
gains `gpuMs`, and the run summary gains `gpuP95Ms`, both absent where the browser has
no timer queries. The GPU-backed headless Chromium that `make bench` uses exposes the
extension.

Tests: `tests/gpu-timer.test.ts` (absent without the extension; results in order once
finished; disjoint frames dropped; bounded in flight; dispose frees everything),
`tests/bench.test.ts` (warm-up GPU times ignored; none reported without the
extension), and `tests/bench-runs.test.ts` (parsed and summarised by median pass).
Gates: lint; 627 unit tests; 53/53 Chromium browser tests (2 workers).

### Baseline runs

These wait for the user to say the laptop is idle (their rule from 2026-09-26). On
2026-09-28 the load average was 11–21 from other work. The runs to do: loaded
(three Ace opponents) on Low, Medium and High, the High preset at device pixel ratio
2, and Corniche at night.

## P8-C1 — Kerbs you feel

### Part 1: a kerb plan, and the verge

Kerbs used to be one band around the whole lap. `src/simulation/kerbs.ts` now plans
separate kerbs from the track's corners. A corner is any run tighter than 300 m
turning 15° or more, as the trackside and the map count them. Each corner gets:

- a kerb on the inside around the apex;
- a kerb on the outside from late in the corner through the exit;
- for corners tighter than 120 m, a kerb on the outside at turn-in.

Kerbs that nearly touch join into one, gaps of 2 m or more stay open, and none is
within 10 m of the start line. Each corner varies its kerbs from a fixed hash, so a
track gets the same plan on every load. The types are:

| Type    | Height | Profile                        | Placement                               |
| ------- | ------ | ------------------------------ | --------------------------------------- |
| Flat    | 30 mm  | Slopes up from the road        | Anywhere                                |
| Stepped | 50 mm  | Rises on its outer part        | Mostly exits                            |
| Sausage | 75 mm  | Rounded hump at the outer edge | Some exits of corners tighter than 80 m |

Widths vary from 60% to 100% of the band. `kerbHeightM` gives the profile, and both
ends ramp down over 1.5 m. The heights follow real F1 kerbs, as the user asked:
realistic, not overdone. Per track: Harbour 30 kerbs, Riviera 47, Ardennes 47, Royal
Park 23, Corniche 45, Test Loop 14, covering 10–33% of each side, with all three types
on every track.

Where the band has no kerb, it is the **verge**: asphalt inside track limits, gripping
like the road. The first try made the gaps grass. Ace then left the road in corners
that had no planned kerb on that side, with tyres up to about 0.4 m past the edge. For
example, on Harbour it left over 190 m of a 200 m-radius sweep. Kerbs from the
minimum-curvature line's edge contacts would place them exactly, but that line costs
30–200 ms per track, per session. The verge is how modern circuits treat the strip
anyway: track limits are unchanged, and grass and gravel start where they did.
`gripSurface` and `isOnTrack` in `trackside.ts` now say once what each surface grips
like and whether it is within limits. The session, guidance, the control API's
off-track events, line tuning and the AI tests all use them.

- Kerb rumble counts only the side with a kerb there, as wide as that kerb.
- Until C2 draws the 3D kerbs, the kerb strips are painted only where kerbs are
  planned, over a light-grey verge.
- Physics version p3.11 → p3.12. The lines were re-tuned (`make tune-lines`, 26 s).
  Ace laps: Harbour 66.400 → 66.035 s, Corniche 104.365 → 104.140 s, Riviera
  77.083 → 77.107 s, and the others within 0.05 s.

Tests: `tests/kerbs.test.ts` (on every track, kerbs only where it turns, separate with
gaps, within the band at real heights, clear of the start; varied types and widths;
deterministic; lookup across the lap's wrap; the profile's ramps and shapes, never above
the kerb's height), `tests/trackside.test.ts` (kerb only on a planned kerb, verge
elsewhere, on Harbour and Riviera; grip and track-limit meaning of each surface), and
`tests/sound-model.test.ts` (rumble only on a side with a kerb, as wide as it is).
Gates: lint; 662 unit tests; 53/53 Chromium browser tests (2 workers).

### Part 2: kerb colliders

`kerbMeshes` cuts each planned kerb into chunks of at most 8 m. Each chunk is a
triangle mesh on the kerb's profile, with vertices every 0.5 m along it and 14 across
it. They all face up, and each chunk records its centre and reach. C2 draws the same
meshes, so what a wheel rides on is what the eye sees.

`src/simulation/kerb-field.ts` makes the chunks within 20 m of the car into Rapier
trimesh colliders, refreshed every 4 m. A trimesh cannot change shape, so chunks are
created on arrival and removed on leaving, not moved as the barrier boxes are. Kerbs
are in their own collision group: the wheels' rays hit them, the chassis passes over.
Every session (player, opponents, headless) gets its track's kerbs.

**Rounded edges.** The first profiles had square steps: a 12 mm lip at the road edge,
and a stepped kerb's upper tier rising within 6 cm. Each wheel of Rapier's raycast
vehicle is a single ray that cannot roll over a step, so a step jolts it in one step.
Crossing a stepped kerb at 150 km/h and 20° lifted the body 0.13 m, and a sausage at 45°
lifted it 0.2 m. The lip is now 15% of the height, and the upper tier rises smoothly
from 50% to 75% of the width, as real kerbs' rounded edges do. Flat and stepped kerbs
then move the body at most 0.10 m and never lift all four wheels. A sausage still
unsettles the car, which is its purpose, but by at most 0.19 m and 0.07 s in the air.

**What "safe" means,** from the car's dimensions (wheel radius 0.36 m, suspension
travel 0.08 m), not from the results:

- every crossing tilts less than 16°;
- the car keeps 90% of its speed on the throttle;
- flat and stepped kerbs move the body less than the suspension travel plus the
  kerb's height, and never lift all four wheels;
- a sausage lifts the body less than a wheel's radius, and all four wheels for at
  most 0.1 s.

A first set of limits ("no wheel off for 0.25 s", "climb under 1.5 m/s") was too
strict: a stiffly sprung car lifts one wheel while straddling a kerb.

- Headless simulation on Harbour: 600× → 520× real time (median of three, on a busy
  laptop).
- Physics p3.12 → p3.13; lines re-tuned. Ace laps: Ardennes 120.629 → 120.490 s,
  Harbour 66.035 → 66.154 s, Royal Park 92.152 → 92.233 s, and the rest within 0.04 s.
  Riviera's tuner lowered one more stretch.

Tests: `tests/kerbs.test.ts` (meshes cover each kerb in chunks of at most 8 m, lie on
its profile on its side, face up, and know their reach) and `tests/kerb-physics.test.ts`
(a car on a 5 cm plateau rests 5 cm higher with every wheel on it; flat, stepped and
sausage kerbs at 50, 150 and 250 km/h and at 5°, 20° and 45°; braking across a
sausage; reversing over a stepped kerb; only nearby chunks solid, and a reset brings
back the start's). Gates: lint; 697 unit tests; 53/53 Chromium browser tests
(2 workers).
